# Visual planning rules

Use these rules to make a scene plan actionable without turning it into a second storyboard.

## Space and continuity

- Define the screen axis only when two or more subjects, travel direction, or object interaction makes it meaningful.
- Track subject zones, facing, gaze, entry and exit, continuing motion, and held-object state only to the precision supported by the fixed context.
- Preserve an incoming state before proposing an outgoing state. A proposal may arrange source facts but may not replace them.
- Establish geography when the audience could not otherwise understand the next action. Avoid redundant coverage when relationships are already readable.

## Coverage and rhythm

- A coverage intention exists to reveal information, register a receiver response, isolate evidence, clarify action, preserve continuity, or shape a supported hook.
- Important dialogue needs a readable performance and, when consequential, a readable receiver response; it does not automatically require shot/reverse-shot.
- Inserts and reactions are justified by a distinct contribution, not by a target shot count.
- Allocate duration according to dialogue, performable action, reaction, and spatial travel. Short and long passages are both valid when supported by the current profile.
- Camera or subject movement is a planning choice only when it changes attention, pressure, recognition, release, geography, or consequence.

## Provenance

These are original project rules adapted from concepts reviewed in `shuohao-skills@0e5eb688` (Apache-2.0) and `drama-skills@7811065` (MIT). They do not reproduce upstream text, fixed cut counts, fixed 2–5 second cuts, file outputs, or provider-specific formats.
